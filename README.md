# AMQ Tours Gatcha

A collection game for the AMQ Tours community: earn credits, collect characters and seiyuus, bid on community auctions, and predict tour results.

UI copy uses book-style title case for page and section headings, navigation, game names, table headings and dialog titles (for example, “Gem Faceting” and “Rules & Scoring”). Short articles, conjunctions and prepositions stay lowercase within titles. Preserve the spelling of player names, anime titles and card names supplied by their sources; descriptions and action sentences use sentence case.

**[Open the shared Cloudflare game](https://play.amqtours-gatcha.workers.dev/)** · [GitHub Pages demo](https://arpia97.github.io/AMQToursGatcha/) · [Deploy the shared game](DEPLOY.md) · [Host tool / API](HOST-TOOLS.md)

The GitHub Pages site is an explicitly labelled **browser-only demo** with fictional players. It does not award real community credits. The same frontend and the shared backend are deployed together on Cloudflare Workers. No paid database, separate server, or custom domain is required for a small deployment within Cloudflare's free allowances.

Gem faceting has ten **Brilliance** nodes and ten **Fractures** nodes. Starting a gem is free; each cut costs 50 credits (1,000 for all 20). Success starts at 75%, decreases 10 percentage points after any success down to 25%, and increases 10 after any failure up to 75%. All 20 nodes must be cut before starting another gem; unfinished gems persist between visits. The final cut automatically pays `max(0, 45 × (Brilliance successes² − Fracture successes²))`: 6/4 pays 900, 7/3 pays 1,800, 8/2 pays 2,700, and 10/0 pays 4,500. Subtract the 1,000 cutting cost for net profit. Exact dynamic programming over 18,879 reachable states gives a maximum mean gross payout of about 879.55 credits under the optimal row-choice policy for these fixed 20-cut rules; it is not a guaranteed individual result.

Coin flip accepts a whole-credit bet from 1 to 1,000,000 within the available wallet. Heads/tails are equally likely. A win pays `floor(1.75 × stake)` including the stake; a loss pays 0. Fractional winning payouts round down, so a 3-credit bet pays 5. Both games use the server's secure random source, atomic wallet mutations, per-game revisions and/or idempotent receipts. Costs and payouts affect net gambling rankings without inflating Cumulative earned credits; an uncut, zero-stake gem does not enter those rankings.

Mahjong uses a standard 136-tile set with one red five per suit replacing a normal five, one dora indicator, 14 reserved dead-wall tiles and 39 unseen opponent tiles. The opening hand has 13 sorted tiles plus the drawn tile, leaving 69 live wall tiles. Each paid discard draws one tile for the user and consumes up to three more for simulated opponents, so Turns left starts at 18 including the partial last turn. Suited tile names use man, pin and sou. Simulated opponents only draw unseen tiles; they do not call or win. This solo variant has no opponent tile calls; it awards no heavenly/earthly-hand bonus. Last-tile tsumo, seven pairs, thirteen orphans, counted yakuman and multiple/double yakuman are supported; no kiriage mangan, honba or other players’ riichi deposits. At exhaustion, the final discard is resolved automatically without another fee to check the 13-tile hand for tenpai, including seven pairs and thirteen orphans. Tenpai refunds the entry fee paid for that hand exactly once (500 for new hands; 1,000 for older hands); discard fees remain spent. Not tenpai refunds nothing and offers a new hand. Ending early has no refund. Haitei requires the user to draw the actual last live tile, rather than an opponent consuming it. The server stores the hidden wall and validates game/revision on every charged action; idempotent requests prevent duplicate charges and claims. Host player/game resets clear Mahjong hands while leaving the catalogue intact, and alias identity migration preserves them.

Riichi is available when a selected discard leaves tenpai and at least four live tiles remain. The declaration and its normal discard/draw are atomic: 1,000 credits for the Riichi stake plus the usual 75-credit replacement fee. Ordinary Riichi adds 1 han; a first-discard declaration gives Double Riichi (2 han instead); winning on the first subsequent draw also earns Ippatsu (1 han). Further discards are restricted to the drawn tile and expire Ippatsu. Winning returns the player’s own 1,000-credit stake in addition to the scored hand payout. Draws and early endings do not return that stake or carry it forward to another solo hand; the existing tenpai entry-fee refund is independent. New hands reserve hidden ura-dora indicators from the dead wall, evaluated/revealed only when a Riichi tsumo is claimed; the preview excludes ura-dora. Existing pre-feature hands remain playable without an invented ura indicator. Costs, stake returns and total payouts persist in private history and net rankings, with idempotent/stale-version protection. See the [EMA Riichi rules](https://mahjong-europe.org/portal/images/docs/Riichi-rules-2016-EN.pdf) for the declaration, locked-hand, Ippatsu and ura-dora rules.

Tile artwork uses the host-supplied set of 34 SVGs under `public/mahjong-tiles`, shared by hands, the dora indicator and discard history. Tile names remain available to assistive technology and on hover; the images scale without cropping. Clicking a tile selects it; clicking that selected tile again discards it for 300 credits, with the existing button available too. Selection resets after a hand revision, and stale actions/retries cannot double-charge. Current-hand discards remain visible. Private hand history pages through ten completed hands at a time, showing final tiles, dora, discarded tiles, spending, winnings, net result and scoring. Older hands that predate detailed recording show their available summaries; missing tile details are never invented.

## Local development

Requires Node.js 24 and pnpm 11.25 or newer.

```sh
pnpm install
```

Generate a random host token locally and save it in the ignored `.dev.vars` file:

```sh
node --input-type=module -e "import {writeFileSync} from 'node:fs'; import {randomBytes} from 'node:crypto'; writeFileSync('.dev.vars','HOST_TOKEN='+randomBytes(32).toString('hex')+'\n')"
pnpm dev
```

Visit `http://localhost:8787/?demo=1` for the sandbox, `http://localhost:8787/` for the real local backend, and `/host.html` for the host console. Open `.dev.vars` locally to obtain your development token. It is not a production token. Production data starts empty; demo state never seeds the backend automatically.

## Gameplay request costs

Mahjong, faceting, horse-racing, roulette, 67 Blackjack and climbing mutations return a compact authenticated `gameState` snapshot with the current wallet, games, recent ledger and pending auction wins. The browser applies it directly; it does not reload the catalogue, collection, rankings or shared shop after each move. Pachinko's periodic/focus refresh uses `GET /api/game-state`; shared data refreshes when leaving the game room. Hidden Mahjong walls, the Blackjack shoe and its active dealer hole card never enter either response.

Roulette replaces Coin Flip: 18 red, 18 black and one green pocket, paying gross 2× for red/black or 35× for green. Full Animation is the default, with wheel/ball slowdown, pocket bounces and a highlighted stop; System Setting and Reduced Motion remain selectable. Mountain Climbing costs 500 credits for ten choices and pays 18 credits per 10 m of final height, rounded down. Random routes include every whole metre. Saved offers and pending spins survive refresh; animations run locally. See [PACHINKO-GAMES.md](PACHINKO-GAMES.md) for exact odds, balance calculations, persistence and API details.

The former 120-actions-per-15-minutes gameplay limit is removed. A per-player in-memory token bucket permits a burst of 30 requests and replenishes 6 per second; it protects against accidental request floods without a long gameplay lockout. Search has a separate 60-request burst, replenishing 5 per second, scoped to the signed-in player or anonymous IP. These guards return a short `Retry-After`; persistent login/registration brute-force protection remains unchanged. Resource-budget counters checkpoint in batches rather than writing on every poll. Economic writes, receipts and game state remain durable and atomic.

A local Workers-runtime benchmark with 601 players, 2,000 catalogue cards, 1,000 owned cards and 10,000 ledger entries measured ten discards after warm-up. Mean rows read per discard including its view update fell from 5,053 to 62 (98.8% fewer); SQL calls fell from 1,077 to 16, and JSON response text from approximately 301 KB to 5.2 KB. This synthetic workload demonstrates the request-path improvement; production costs vary with account data. Cloudflare's account-wide free limits still apply.

## Architecture

`public/` is a buildless, responsive frontend. `src/worker.js` serves the API and static assets. One SQLite-backed Durable Object owns the community economy. `src/engine.js` executes economic mutations synchronously inside transactions, preventing concurrent overspending. Mutations require an idempotency key. Passwords use salted PBKDF2; session tokens are hashed in storage and sent in HttpOnly SameSite cookies. Every host request checks the current administrator role and either canonical owner ID 368 or a persisted owner-issued delegation. Arbitrary flags cannot authorize host requests. Role revocation takes effect immediately, and delegation changes are audit logged. The console does not offer token unlock; the owner-held deployment secret is retained for trusted external integrations and recovery. Same-origin JSON requests and login throttles protect mutation endpoints.

This design targets an AMQ community, not an unlimited global service. Catalog browsing/search and user listings are paginated (40 cards), rankings use 50-player pages, and pick’em history uses 20-match pages. Shared state contains only referenced card metadata. Bulk metadata uses bounded SQLite JSON chunks to keep initial row writes low. Profile search shows up to 50 matches. Free service allowances and upstream image availability still apply.

Collection and search inspiration: [karitham/WaifuBot](https://github.com/karitham/WaifuBot). This is an independent implementation of the requested AMQ economy, rather than a deployment of that Discord bot.

Collection controls include independent rarity and character-role filters plus sort choices, with 40 cards per page and navigation above and below the cards. Anime progress sorts pinned anime first, then completion percentage. Card Alchemy opens a searchable one-material picker, with separate target selection per tier across both card types. Card and avatar frame colours follow rarity, with equal base thickness and glow at every rarity; enhancement increases thickness and adds a level-coloured circling aura. Sealed packs with A-or-lower best pulls have a softer glow. Auction countdowns sit in section headers; auction cards link the current highest bidder.

Paginated card grids use column counts that divide their full page sizes: 40-card catalogue/collection/marketplace pages and 24-card seller/material pages, with 12 alchemy targets. Short final pages remain naturally incomplete. Empty and selected alchemy material slots share portrait proportions.

Owned cards reveal actions on hover or touch. Shop purchases refill atomically with a new listing ID, preserving the current weekly deadline or active flash window.

Actions stays beside Enhance while its full-width menu expands below. Card packs and reveal screens offer System / Full / Reduced animation preferences, saved per browser; Full explicitly enables pack effects even when the system requests reduced motion. Lower-tier glow strength remains subdued. New players receive 5,000 starting credits, and identities previously given 1,000 receive the 4,000 difference exactly once. Premium character and seiyuu packs default to 1,000 each.

Collection tabs, search, sorting and rarity/role filters share a compact responsive toolbar. Pagination sits directly below the controls, with a separate 36px gap before the cards and additional space around the lower pager.

Concealed Kan is available for four matching held tiles, including red/normal fives as the same face. It reveals an additional dora indicator and draws a free replacement from the saved dead wall. One live-wall tail tile is sealed to maintain 14 dead-wall tiles; no opponent draws are simulated for this extra draw. Up to four Kans are supported. Rinshan kaihou applies to a winning replacement, without Haitei; any Kan cancels Ippatsu. After Riichi, only a drawn fourth tile is eligible and the library verifies unchanged wait sets and all winning interpretations. Later discards use the shortened concealed hand’s last tile. Concealed Kans preserve a closed hand. Older hands without a recorded dead wall remain playable but cannot Kan. Red fives contribute Aka-dora as well as matching ordinary dora/ura indicators.

Mahjong retains detailed state for the latest 50 games per player, including the current hand. Older outcome JSON and hand payloads inside retry receipts are compacted; financial stake/payout/net rows and receipt fingerprints/keys stay intact for lifetime rankings and replay safety. Existing history is compacted once during deployment initialization, then each new hand enforces the limit. Current Mahjong pricing is 500 to start and 75 per discard.

## Quiz

Quiz stays at Coming Soon until a host publishes a pool. Host Console → Quiz imports AnisongDB JSON arrays or AMQ song exports with nested `songInfo`, optionally uploads a PNG/JPEG/WebP cover, and sets a perfect-score title. AMQ exports without audio links resolve exact ANN occurrence IDs (or explicit shared AMQ song IDs) through AnisongDB in sequential batches of up to 500, with a bounded 24-hour in-memory cache. ### Worker Recovery and Request Cost

Read-only production logs showed Cloudflare internal Durable Object storage resets and overlong request queues across login, state, notices and catalogue calls. Safe GET forwarding recreates the stub and retries once with jitter; overloaded errors and all POST requests are never automatically replayed. Exhausted storage failures return a retryable JSON 503 rather than an HTML Worker error. This does not claim to eliminate a Cloudflare infrastructure incident. Browser GETs coalesce while in flight; background full refresh runs every two minutes, is coalesced across focus/timers, backs off after a failure, and pauses together with notice polling during an active quiz or preload. Ownership-count queries use card indexes for marketplace/auction/trade escrow, including bundled and legacy offers. Market maintenance coalesces within five seconds while still honoring exact deadlines and forcing fresh checks after market mutations. The original Durable Object identity, catalogue, importer cadence, economy and scheduled market reform are retained.

Catalogue text searches narrow packed rows in SQL before JSON parsing, rarity decoration or sorting, instead of building the complete card pool for each search. Both browsing keeps global ordering and pagination across matches; non-ASCII queries retain JavaScript case matching. Unfiltered browse and draw pools retain their existing caches.
